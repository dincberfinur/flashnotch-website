Your Anki reps live in the notch

A glanceable due count sits in your MacBook notch or a floating bar. Hover, flip, grade, and return to your work. FlashNotch drives your real Anki, so your schedule stays exactly as Anki computes it.

01 How it works

Hover, flip, grade

01

Glance

The notch shows today's due count, straight from Anki and already limit-adjusted. A green check means you are caught up.

02

Expand

Hover the notch or press ⌥⌘R. The panel drops out with your real card: images, cloze, MathJax, and audio.

03

Grade

Space flips, 1–4 grades, Z undoes. Every answer lands in Anki's scheduler the moment you press it.

04 Details

Small app, sharp edges filed off

Follows your Mac: light, dark, or system

The panel follows your Mac's appearance, or you can pick Light or Dark in Settings. Cards keep your note type's own night-mode styling, so they look the way they do in Anki. In dark mode, the panel turns pure black and fuses with the notch.

  • Light, Dark, or System set in the app
  • Cards follow Anki's night-mode classes
  • Pure-black panel in dark mode, fused with the notch

Pick a deck, review its whole subtree

The picker shows your deck tree with today's due counts, straight from Anki and already limit-adjusted. Pick a parent to review everything under it, or a single subdeck. You review under Anki's daily limits, same as the desktop app.

  • Counts respect your daily limits, not the raw backlog
  • Parents count their whole subtree, the way Anki does
  • When a deck finishes, the next deck with cards waiting is one click away

Quiet by design

No bouncing badges, no streak guilt, no notifications. One number in the notch, and a green check when a deck is done. FlashNotch never pings you; you look when you want to.

  • No push notifications
  • No streaks or gamification pressure
  • A green check and today's count when a deck is done

No notch? Use the floating bar

On a Mac without a notch, or on an external display, FlashNotch shows the same due count in a small floating bar. Drag it wherever you like. The panel opens right from it, and you can drag the open panel too.

  • Automatic picks the notch or the bar for each display
  • Position is remembered between launches
  • Pick Notch or Floating Bar yourself in Settings
05 The small stuff

More in the margins

Real card rendering

Images, audio, cloze, typed answers, and MathJax render the way they do in Anki, because it is your actual card.

Hover to expand

A short hover opens the panel and moving away closes it. Prefer the keyboard? Turn hover off and use ⌥⌘R.

Zoom and resize

⌘+ and ⌘− zoom the card. Drag the bottom-right corner to make the panel the size you like.

Scroll to review

Prefer the trackpad? Swipe down to flip, down again for Good, up for Again. Optional, with three sensitivity levels.

Mark and edit in Anki

Press M to mark a note for later, or E to open it in Anki's editor. Sync with AnkiWeb from the ⋯ menu.

Optional bury add-on

A free companion add-on, included with the app, adds bury (− and =) using Anki's own operation. Strictly optional.

Quick fixes

  • “Anki isn’t running” Open Anki and leave it running in the background.
  • “One free add-on to connect” In Anki, choose Tools → Add-ons → Get Add-ons, paste 2055492159, then restart Anki.
  • “Anki is asleep” Click Anki’s window once to wake it, then try again.
  • Images don’t load Open Settings → Anki → Choose Folder and pick your Anki2 folder again.
  • Nothing in the notch Use Open Review Panel in the menu bar, or press ⌥⌘R.
